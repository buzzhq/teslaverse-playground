Overview

Sum of the parts: we value each Tesla business on its own (cars, energy storage, robotaxi, Optimus robots, FSD software), add the cash Tesla holds minus its debt, then divide by the number of shares. Each business is valued with a DCF: guess its cash flow for 10 years, add a "terminal value" for everything after, and shrink future dollars back to today's using a discount rate.

Starter settings reproduce the Teslaverse Valuation Model v4 (cars, energy, robotaxi, cash = US$50.30) and add Optimus and FSD, which live only on this site. Tap Explain ▾ under any slider.

    Optimus

    • Production went from a few dozen a week (Q2 2026) to several hundred a week (Aug 2026); a >1,000/week line is targeted by end-2026 (The Information via Electrek).
    • No price, orders or release date published; first robots are used inside Tesla. Musk targets public sales in late 2027.

    • Model: price starts at US$60k (assumption), falling each year; every Optimus number is an assumption.

    FSD (Full Self-Driving)

    • 1.48m active paid FSD customers at 30 Jun 2026 (+56% in a year); ~55% bought upfront, ~45% subscribe.
    • Subscription-only since 14 Feb 2026: US$99/month. Approved in the US, Canada, Australia, NZ, parts of Europe; China launched May 2026.
    • Still "Supervised": the driver must watch the road. No licensing deal signed with any carmaker.
    • Avoiding double counting: today's FSD revenue is already inside car revenue (and the car margin), so only subscribers above today's ~0.67m monthly subscribers count here. Upfront purchases stay in Cars. Robotaxi fares are valued only in the Robotaxi section, and Optimus has no overlap.

    Beyond Tesla

    Two separate companies linked to the Teslaverse. They are not part of Tesla's value per share or build-up above; each has its own headline, sliders and checks.

    SpaceX + xAI

    SpaceX bought xAI on 2 Feb 2026 (SpaceX US$1tn + xAI US$250bn = US$1.25tn), then listed on Nasdaq as SPCX on 12 Jun 2026 at US$135 (US$1.77tn). Because they are one listed company, we compare our sum of the parts with SPCX's market value (US$146.03 at 16 Oct 2026 close x 13.08bn IPO shares), not two private valuations.

    Value by part

    What this shows: Starlink already makes real cash; xAI loses money for years before (maybe) turning positive. Years are calendar 2027-2036.

    What this shows: only two quarters are public since the IPO filing. Starlink went from 10.3m to 12.0m subscribers between March and June 2026. Q1 launch revenue is derived (total minus the other segments).

    • Tesla links: SpaceX bought about US$295m of Tesla Megapacks in Q2 2026 (US$329m in H1) to power AI data centres, recorded by Tesla as related-party revenue. Musk runs both companies, and xAI is a big buyer of Nvidia chips.
    • Net cash is not modelled here (IPO proceeds vs debt and heavy AI spending), so treat values as enterprise-level.

    Nvidia

    The world's main supplier of AI chips. Why it matters to the Teslaverse: Tesla trains FSD and Optimus AI on Nvidia hardware alongside its own chips, and xAI's Colossus data centres run on Nvidia GPUs. Base year = Q2 FY2027 (quarter to 26 Jul 2026) x 4.

    What this shows: projected free cash flow at the slider settings; with cautious 15% growth, cash flow roughly doubles in 5 years.

    What this shows: data-centre sales have more than tripled in two years, while gross margin has stayed around 73-75%. From FY2027 Nvidia reports only Data Center and Edge Computing, so gaming is no longer shown separately. Missing margin points were not checked against filings.
