Overview

Sum of the parts: we value each Tesla business on its own (cars, energy storage, robotaxi, Optimus robots, FSD software), add the cash Tesla holds minus its debt, then divide by the number of shares. Each business is valued with a DCF: guess its cash flow for 10 years, add a "terminal value" for everything after, and shrink future dollars back to today's using a discount rate.

Starter settings reproduce the Teslaverse Valuation Model v4 (cars, energy, robotaxi, cash = US$50.30) and add Optimus and FSD, which live only on this site. Tap Explain ▾ under any slider.

    SpaceX (incl. xAI): Overview

    A separate company from Tesla, not part of Tesla's value. SpaceX bought xAI on 2 Feb 2026 (SpaceX US$1tn + xAI US$250bn = US$1.25tn), then went public in June 2026: 638.9m Class A shares sold at US$135 (including the full over-allotment) for net proceeds of US$85.7bn. The legal name is Space Exploration Technologies Corp. ("SpaceX" is the trade name); xAI is a wholly owned subsidiary, X.AI Holdings, which also owns X. Ticker SPCX on Nasdaq and Nasdaq Texas. Because it is one listed company, we compare our sum of the parts plus net cash with SPCX's market value (US$146.03 at 16 Oct 2026 close x 13.18bn shares).

    Value by part

    What this shows: on these cautious settings the parts add up to a small fraction of what the market pays. The market is betting on much faster Starlink growth, a winning xAI and big Starship-era businesses.

    What this shows: Starlink already makes real cash; xAI loses money for years before (maybe) turning positive. Years are calendar 2027-2036.

    Trends

    What this shows: revenue grew from US$10.4bn (2023) to US$14.0bn (2024) and US$18.7bn (2025), almost all from Starlink, which went from 37% to 61% of the total. In Q2 2026 revenue hit US$7.8bn in one quarter (up 92% on a year earlier) as AI revenue jumped to US$2.6bn. Left of the dashed line are full years, right are single quarters, so the bars are not the same scale.

    Shared settings

    Starlink

    Satellite internet: 12.0m subscribers paying ~US$66/month (Q2 2026); the only profitable segment in Q1 2026. Value = subscribers x ARPU x margin.

    Trends

    What this shows: subscribers roughly doubled every year: 2.3m (end-2023), 4.4m (2024), 8.9m (2025), 12.0m (Jun 2026). The average monthly bill (ARPU) fell from US$99 to US$66 as Starlink moved into cheaper countries and plans.

    What this shows: Connectivity revenue tripled from US$3.9bn (2023) to US$11.4bn (2025); Q2 2026 was US$4.3bn (up 66%). Operating margin rose from 12% (2023) to 39% (2025) and was 39% again in Q2 2026. Enterprise & government (airlines, ships, Starshield) is the fastest-growing part.

    Sources: SpaceX 424(b)(4) prospectus (12 Jun 2026) for 2023-2025 and Q1/Q2 2025; Q2 2026 10-Q and 4 Aug 2026 release for 2026. Q3 and Q4 2025 were never reported separately, so the chart jumps from full-year 2025 to 2026 quarters.

    Launch

    Falcon 9 / Dragon flights for outside customers (Space segment US$0.96bn in Q2 2026; 165 Falcon launches in 2025).

    Trends

    What this shows: SpaceX launched 98 times in 2023, 138 in 2024 and 170 in 2025 (Falcon 96, 134, 165; the rest are Starship test flights: 2, 4 and 5). Only a quarter to a third are for paying customers (33, 45, 43); most carry SpaceX's own Starlink satellites. Quarterly totals: 38, 46, 40, 38.

    What this shows: outside-customer Space revenue is fairly flat at US$3.6-4.1bn a year, and the segment slipped from break-even to a US$0.66bn loss in 2025 as Starship development spending rose. Q2 2026 revenue was US$0.96bn (up 29%), with a US$0.54bn operating loss.

    Assumptions

    xAI

    Grok chatbot, X (Twitter) and the Colossus data centres. AI revenue US$2.56bn in Q2 2026, but large losses. Three stories weighted by chance.

    Key trends

    • Revenue finally took off in Q2 2026: US$2.56bn, up 3.5x on Q2 2025, driven by new compute-rental (Cloud Services) deals. Before that, AI revenue was flat at about US$3bn a year in 2023-2025.
    • X advertising is shrinking: US$2.32bn (2023), US$1.73bn (2024), US$1.84bn (2025), and US$367m in Q2 2026 (down 14% on a year earlier).
    • Huge build-out: AI capex went from US$0.5bn (2023) to US$12.7bn (2025) and US$15.8bn in Q2 2026 alone; compute power reached 1.4 GW. Operating losses continue (US$6.4bn in 2025), but adjusted EBITDA turned positive at US$1.1bn in Q2 2026.

    Trends

    What this shows: until 2026 the AI segment was mostly X's ad business. "AI solutions & infrastructure" (Grok subscriptions, X subscriptions, API and compute rental) grew from US$0.64bn (2023) to US$1.36bn (2025), then US$2.19bn in Q2 2026 alone, of which US$1.6bn came from new compute contracts.

    What this shows: the AI segment has lost money every period. The 2023 loss includes a US$3.8bn impairment charge. The adjusted EBITDA line strips out depreciation of the GPUs, which is now the biggest cost, so it looks much better than the operating line.

    What this shows: spending on data centres has exploded: 2025 capex was 27x 2023, and the first half of 2026 (US$23.6bn) already beats all of 2025. Power ("nameplate compute") went from zero at end-2023 to 0.3 GW (end-2024), 0.8 GW (end-2025) and 1.4 GW (Jun 2026).

    • Colossus GPUs (reporting): Colossus 1 in Memphis went live Jul 2024 with up to 100,000 Nvidia H100s (DCD, Nvidia, Oct 2024); doubled to 200,000 by about Feb 2025 (xAI). Colossus II (Memphis and Southaven, MS) is a gigawatt-scale cluster; in 2026 SpaceX agreed to rent about 325,000 Nvidia GPUs across Colossus and Colossus II to one customer for US$1.25bn a month to May 2029 (prospectus). No official total GPU count for 2026.
    • Users (prospectus): about 520m monthly active users across Grok and X at Dec 2025 and 550m at Mar 2026; of these, 89m and 117m used Grok's AI features. No earlier MAU history and no Q2 2026 figure was published.

    Sources: SpaceX 424(b)(4) prospectus (12 Jun 2026) for 2023-2025 and Q1/Q2 2025; Q2 2026 10-Q and 4 Aug 2026 release for 2026. Q3 and Q4 2025 were never reported separately, so the chart jumps from full-year 2025 to 2026 quarters.
