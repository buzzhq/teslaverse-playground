Overview

Sum of the parts: we value each Tesla business on its own (cars, energy storage, robotaxi, Optimus robots, FSD software), add the cash Tesla holds minus its debt, then divide by the number of shares. Each business is valued with a DCF: guess its cash flow for 10 years, add a "terminal value" for everything after, and shrink future dollars back to today's using a discount rate.

Starter settings reproduce the Teslaverse Valuation Model v4 method (cars, energy, robotaxi, cash = US$50.77 after the Q3 2026 cash/share update; was US$50.30) and add Optimus and FSD, which live only on this site. Tap Explain ▾ under any slider.

    Starship / other

    14 test flights since Apr 2023; Flight 14 (28 Sep 2026) reached orbit and deployed 26 Starlink V3 satellites. No outside Starship revenue yet. Valued as a simple option: chance x payoff.

    Key trends

    • 14 test flights in 3.5 years, getting better: 4 failures in the first 8 flights, none in the last 6 (7 successes, 3 partials overall). Flight 14 (28 Sep 2026) reached orbit for the first time and deployed 26 real Starlink V3 satellites.

    • Spending is rising fast: Space segment R&D (mostly Starship) went from US$1.5bn (2023) to US$3.0bn (2025) and US$1.08bn in Q2 2026 alone.

    Milestones ahead

    • Ship-to-ship propellant transfer (orbital refuelling): planned for Mar 2025, then Mar 2026; GAO (Jul 2026) still lists it for 2026; not flown as of 1 Oct 2026. Needs two ships in orbit - only possible since Flight 14.

    • Booster and ship catches/reuse: SpaceX's near-term goals include reaching orbit, catching and reusing ships and boosters (Q2 2026 call); Flight 15 (Booster 22 / Ship 42) was being readied for about late Oct 2026 (NASASpaceflight).
    • NASA Artemis III (2027): crewed test in low Earth orbit docking Orion with Starship HLS and Blue Moon pathfinders; "on track for 2027, but anything can happen" (Reuters, 5 Oct 2026).
    • Artemis IV (2028): first crewed Moon landing of the program, which needs proven refuelling.
    • Orbital AI compute satellites: "as early as 2028" (prospectus).

    Tesla's big bets: if it works vs. what it is worth today

    Robotaxi, Optimus and FSD licensing are bets: huge if they work, little if they don't. A fair value today multiplies each outcome by its chance and adds them up (the probability-weighted value). Change the chances on the Robotaxi, Optimus and FSD tabs and this updates.

    What this shows: green = value per share if the big version works; purple = what the model actually counts after weighting by your probabilities. The gap between them is the "risk discount". Log scale, so each gridline is 10x.

    Competition and risks (a few sourced numbers)

    • Waymo (robotaxi): 4,000+ driverless cars and about 500,000 paid rides a week (Robotaxi Report, Oct 2026). Tesla publishes no fleet size; community trackers counted ~1,111 Tesla robotaxis on 7 Oct 2026 (409 unsupervised). Tesla's edge: cheaper cameras-only cars it builds itself; Waymo's edge: years of driverless miles.
    • Tesla robotaxi progress: ~2m cumulative unsupervised miles by 30 Sep 2026 (8x in Q3), Cybercab rides in Austin since 3 Sep, Dallas next (Q3 2026 Update and call). Risk: management says scaling is still limited by software (night, highways, weather) and then regulators.
    • BYD (cars and driver assistance): sold 4.60m new-energy vehicles in 2025 (2.26m fully electric), vs Tesla's 1.64m, and made its "God's Eye" driver assistance standard on most models, some priced under US$10,000 (BYD annual report; Reuters, Feb 2025). Risk: free assistance features in China put pressure on FSD pricing there.
    • Humanoid rivals: Unitree shipped ~5,500 humanoids in 2025 with the G1 from US$13,500; Figure (BMW), Agility Digit and Boston Dynamics Atlas (Hyundai) are in pilots. Tesla has started "early production" in Fremont but sells none yet and has published no price (Q3 2026 call).
    • FSD licensing: no carmaker has signed a licence (Musk, Sep 2026). FSD is still "Supervised" and approved only in some countries; EU-wide approval hoped for by Q1 2027 (Musk's estimate).
    • Key-person and related-party risk: Musk runs Tesla and SpaceX/xAI; Tesla is exploring a possible merger with SpaceX (Reuters/Euronews, Oct 2026) and would not answer questions about it on the call.

    What would it take? (reverse calculator)

    Normal valuation goes assumptions → price. This goes backwards: pick a share price, and see what you would have to believe to get there. It starts from your current slider settings (change them on any Tesla tab, or pick a preset on Overview).

    Default is today's price (US$386.47, 21 Oct 2026 close). Drag to test a higher or lower price.

    What it means: the market price already "contains" a set of beliefs about the future. We search, one lever at a time, for the setting that makes the model equal your target.

    How to read it: "Needed" is the value that lever alone would need. "Not reachable" means even the extreme end of the range is not enough on its own. The bar chart shows how far each lever must move compared with now (2 = double).

    Bulls vs bears: bulls say several bets will work at once (see the last row); bears say even one of these levels is a stretch.

    Caution: these are model outputs, not forecasts. Reality checks use only sourced facts listed on the Sources tab.

    How far each lever must move

    What this shows: each bar is the needed setting divided by today's (for the discount rate, today's divided by needed, since lower helps). Long bars mean big leaps of faith. Bars missing = that lever alone cannot reach the target.
